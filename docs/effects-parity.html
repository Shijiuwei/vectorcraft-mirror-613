The pixel-filter pipeline (vectorcraft_effects::pixel) is in place: each new filter is one function, a catalogue row, a menu entry and translations. Measured throughput: 0.7–1.2 agent hours per filter (see calibration). Every Photoshop-style filter is in the catalogue, and the Effect Gallery dialog shipped (#2883); its filter thumbnails and in-dialog preview area are the 1–2 h that remain.

Film Grain (artistic.filmGrain, Effect › Artistic) adds stable monochrome noise to shadows and midtones, with Grain, Highlight Area and Intensity sliders, live preview and appearance editing. Its smooth grain field follows translations, preserves transparency and stays in document units; zoomed-out rendering uses bounded approximate footprint filtering. Changing the bounds shifts the pattern. Command exports bake it to an image in SVG and PDF, while the native document keeps the effect editable. Behaviour is an original visual approximation based on Illustrator's public effects reference and the public Artistic filters guide, not a pixel-identical proprietary implementation. Intensity is interpreted as highlight strength and quieter highlight grain. Grain strength stays even across shadows and midtones and rolls off above mid-grey to a quarter-strength floor at full Intensity before highlight headroom; cream retains soft grain, black takes light speckles and white stays clean. The tonal curve and endpoint behaviour are interpretations, not verified proprietary behaviour. Saturated colour channels may clip. The parameter ranges and defaults are chosen for VectorCraft, not asserted as measured proprietary defaults.

Cutout, Neon Glow and Poster Edges (artistic.cutout, artistic.neonGlow, artistic.posterEdges) follow the same two public references, with original algorithms. Cutout softens the object's colours (Edge Simplicity, less with Edge Fidelity), splits each colour channel into Number of Levels steps, and paints each resulting piece in the average colour that falls into it, so hues survive. Poster Edges posterizes each colour channel to Posterization + 2 levels and draws black lines on the edges of any channel and of the object's outline, wider with Edge Thickness and darker, on fainter edges too, with Edge Intensity. Neon Glow redraws the object in dimmed greys screened with a glow colour from its light areas (positive Glow Size) or its dark ones (negative), spread by the size and as strong as Glow Brightness; with no foreground and background colours in Illustrator's model, the glow colour is the effect's own parameter. All three keep coverage, work in document units at any Document Raster Effects resolution, and use the generic effect dialog with live preview. Ranges and defaults are chosen for VectorCraft. Evidence: crates/effects/src/pixel/artistic.rs, the artistic_*, cutout_*, poster_edges_* and neon_glow_* tests in crates/effects/src/pixel/tests.rs, and artistic_settings_survive_a_save_and_open in crates/engine/src/tests/rasterfilters.rs. Smudge Stick (artistic.smudgeStick), Sponge (artistic.sponge) and Watercolor (artistic.watercolor), Effect › Artistic, are original visual approximations from the public descriptions in Illustrator's effects reference and the public Artistic filters guide, not pixel-identical copies. Each has live preview, appearance editing, document-unit patterns that follow the object, bounded work per pixel and kept coverage (they paint nothing outside the object), and bakes to an image in SVG and PDF. Ranges and defaults are chosen for VectorCraft, not asserted as measured.

  • Smudge Stick (Stroke Length 0–10, Highlight Area 0–20, Intensity 0–10): dark areas smudged along short diagonal strokes; light areas smudged with strokes twice as long and lightened towards white.
  • Sponge (Brush Size 0–10, Definition 0–25, Smoothness 1–15): the object softened, then lighter and darker blobs of the same hue, their contrast set by Definition, their edges by Smoothness.
  • Watercolor (Brush Detail 1–14, Shadow Intensity 0–10, Texture 1–3): detail simplified by an edge-keeping average whose brush widens as Brush Detail falls, colour saturated and pooled darker where tones change at edges, shadows deepened, a faint paper grain. Edges are found from tone only, so a change of hue at equal brightness is not saturated.

Tests: pixel::tests_paint::*, tests_rasterfilters, dialogs::tests::raster_filter_dialogs_*, tests_labels::effect_menu_lists_the_artistic_filters_* and the command_sweep structured junk.

Near white, highlight headroom can limit grain equally at every Intensity; highlight lift still depends on Intensity when Highlight Area is nonzero. The two rotated, variance-normalised noise layers can leave a faint diagonal weave at high magnification. Removing that remaining direction would require a more expensive field; the current implementation keeps bounded per-pixel work. Film Grain is still slower than Texture Grain (Regular), particularly when its wider footprint needs four samples. Calibration on uniform grey at Grain 20, using 32×32 fine samples per coarse pixel, measured grain standard deviation within about 15% of the dense area average at pixel widths of 2, 4 and 8 document units (ratios 1.13, 0.89, 0.85). This is evidence for the bounded approximation, not an accuracy guarantee for arbitrary artwork. Tests: pixel::tests::film_grain_*, tests_rasterfilters, tests_adjust::film_grain_dialog_* and tests_labels::effect_menu_lists_the_artistic_filters_*.

Bas Relief, Charcoal, Photocopy and Stamp (sketch.basRelief, sketch.charcoal, sketch.photocopy, sketch.stamp, Effect › Sketch; pixel/sketch_ink.rs) redraw the object in black ink on white paper and keep its coverage. Bas Relief carves low relief lit from one of eight directions (Detail, Smoothness, Light Direction); Charcoal draws bold edges and sketches the midtones in diagonal strokes (Thickness, Detail, Light/Dark Balance); Photocopy prints only where a pixel is darker than its surroundings, so large dark areas copy around their edges (Detail, Darkness); Stamp reduces the object to solid black and white (Light/Dark Balance, Smoothness). Sizes are in document points at any Document Raster Effects resolution, strokes and noise lie in document space around the object's centre, and all four preview live. Behaviour is an original visual approximation based on Illustrator's public effects reference and the public Sketch filters guide, not a pixel-identical proprietary implementation; the parameter ranges and defaults are chosen for VectorCraft, not asserted as measured proprietary defaults. Tests: pixel::tests_sketch::*, tests_rasterfilters, command_sweep, dialogs::tests::raster_filter_dialogs_preview_and_apply and tests_labels::effect_menu_lists_the_sketch_filters_*.

Effect › Sketch › Halftone Pattern and Note Paper (sketch.halftonePattern, sketch.notePaper) are original black-and-white filters guided by Illustrator's public Sketch effects summary and effects overview (Halftone Size in points at any Document Raster Effects resolution). The public help pages name each control but publish no numeric defaults or slider limits; VectorCraft's ranges follow those control names and the emboss/halftone behaviour described there, while dialog defaults are chosen for VectorCraft and are not verified against proprietary defaults. Evidence: crates/effects/src/pixel/sketch.rs, halftone_* and note_paper_* in crates/effects/src/pixel/tests.rs, SKETCH and halftone_pattern_settings_survive_a_save_and_open in crates/engine/src/tests/rasterfilters.rs, and tests_labels::effect_menu_lists_the_sketch_filters_between_sharpen_and_stylize.

Effect › Sketch › Conté Crayon (sketch.conteCrayon; pixel/sketch.rs) redraws the object in a dense black crayon where it is dark and a pure white crayon where it is light, on mid-grey paper, as Illustrator's public effects summary describes (black for dark areas, white for light areas). Its controls follow the public Photoshop filter reference and the Photoshop Elements Sketch filters guide (foreground and background levels, texture options): Foreground Level and Background Level (0–20) set how far each crayon reaches into the midtones; Texture (Brick, Burlap, Canvas, Sandstone, the surfaces Texturizer draws), Scaling, Relief, Light Direction and Invert make the paper's tooth, which breaks the diagonal crayon strokes, and its relief. The public pages publish no slider ranges or defaults, so those are unverified VectorCraft choices; an original approximation, never compared with another application's output. Tests: pixel::tests_sketch::conte_crayon_*, tests_rasterfilters, command_sweep, dialogs::tests::raster_filter_dialogs_preview_and_apply and tests_labels::effect_menu_lists_the_sketch_filters_between_sharpen_and_stylize.
