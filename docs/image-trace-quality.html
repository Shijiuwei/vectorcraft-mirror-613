What Image Trace (crates/trace) makes of images whose right answer is known, measured by a harness and compared with the numbers before each change. Nothing here is a judgement by eye, and nothing is compared with any other product except the open tracer named under Yardstick.

Auto Color keeps rare colours

The Automatic palette (Auto Color) counts flat art's colours by area: a colour needs about 1% of the picture (0.5% at Colors 100) to be counted, and the count is then fitted by median cut (Heckbert 1982) and k-means (Lloyd 1982), weighted by area. A stem, a highlight or a cream close to a pale background was averaged into a large neighbour, and Noise could remove what was left of it. Now, on the flat route only, after the count is fitted, the colours too rare or too close for it are found and kept when they are:

  • distinct: at least 2.5 (Oklab × 100; Ottosson 2020) from every palette colour and from the main colour each one stands for;
  • flat: mostly one histogram bin;
  • drawn: a 4-connected region of their own (not blended) pixels of at least 8 pixels, or three of at least 4 (repeated highlights); a colour lying between two others, as an edge's anti-aliasing mixes them, must also have an inside (pixels whose four neighbours are all of it).

At most six are kept, largest region first. They are fixed while the other colours are refitted around them, mixed-colour merging leaves them alone, and Noise removal keeps their regions of 4 pixels or more (smaller specks of them still go). This follows the idea of salient-colour preservation in palette reduction (Fukuda et al. 2019, doi:10.1117/12.2521607); the rule and its numbers are VectorCraft's own. Full Tone, Limited and the continuous-tone route of Automatic are unchanged.

Tests (code-drawn): automatic_keeps_a_rare_distinct_stem_or_patch (2, 3, 5 and 8 flat colours and a contrasting stem 1–4 px wide or a patch, about 0.05% to 0.5% of the picture, hard and anti-aliased at 1× and 2×: the stem's colour is kept and no other is added), automatic_keeps_near_white_highlights_on_a_pale_background (ten white islands of about 12 px on #FFF4E0: kept, each still a path at Noise 16), automatic_keeps_a_thin_low_contrast_stem (#909090 on #808080, 3 px), scattered_noise_adds_no_colour (the negative control: the same areas as single scattered pixels add no colour) and noise_keeps_a_kept_rare_colours_regions_but_not_its_specks (Noise 0 to 32).

Measured on 22 synthetic flat-colour logos drawn from known palettes, each hard 1×, anti-aliased 1× and 2× (66 inputs, used locally and not committed; a key colour is missing when no traced colour is within ΔE00 5): every key colour and no spurious one in 66 of 66 (was 39); missing key colours 0 (was 43), spurious 0 (was 2), key colours sharing one traced colour 0 pairs (was 46); mean rendered ΔE00 against the input 0.138 (was 0.348); paths 2547 → 2586, anchors 27,204 → 24,896, trace time 2.85 → 3.02 s in all. On four public-domain scan crops (1024 × 1024) Auto Color takes the continuous-tone route and nothing changes: 14, 72, 199 and 98 colours, the same paths, anchors and rendered error (2.11, 5.66, 9.15 and 1.00 ΔE00), time within run-to-run spread.

Collapsed loops keep few segments

A small closed loop (or a piece that returns to its own junction) whose fit collapsed to a single cubic cannot stay as one, so it kept every positioned point as a straight segment. A looser Paths collapses more loops, so it could give more segments than a tighter one. Such a loop now tries the fewest segments that still hold it, each only if the result keeps an area (more than 0.25 px²) and stays within 1.5 × max(tolerance, 0.5 px) of the points both ways: the fit at up to three somewhat tighter tolerances (what a slightly higher Paths gives); for a closed loop then two cubics split at its start and its farthest point, then the outline simplified at the tolerance and finer; the positioned points last, as before. Test on code-drawn images: a_higher_paths_never_gives_fewer_segments (32 seeded 48 px images of soft blobs in four colours plus pixel noise, Color and Black and White, Noise 1 and 3, segments at Paths 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 must not fall). Before, six runs fell, e.g. 125 → 79 segments from Paths 0 to 25 (seed 21, Color, Noise 1) and 71 → 63 → 52 from Paths 0 to 50 (seed 3, Black and White, Noise 3); now they are 75 → 79 and 41 → 48 → 52. One image (seed 16 in Black and White) still falls from 140 to 136 segments between Paths 0 and 25, before and after this change; that fall has another cause, still open, and the image is left out of the test. The crate's other tests pass unchanged.

Notches read only their own piece

Where the pixel at a sharp corner falls to the other colour, the outline cuts the corner in two one-pixel steps, and between two straight edges of three cracks or more those two steps become one point, where the two edges meet (a notch). Whether the edges were long enough was measured on the whole loop. An edge can run on past a junction in one region's loop while the region across it, whose loop turns at that junction, sees a shorter edge: one side made the notch and the other did not, the shared piece got different points on each side, and the abutting curves left a gap. The rule in subpixel::outline now also requires the three cracks of each edge next to the notch to lie in the piece (no junction among them), as the corner-pixel correction above it already did. Test on code-drawn images: noisy_colour_images_leave_no_sample_uncovered (20 seeded 36 px images of soft overlapping blobs in four colours plus pixel noise; Color, 5 colours, Noise 1 and 2, Paths 0, 50 and 100; five sample points per pixel must each lie in some path). Without the condition 93 sample points were uncovered (seeds 9, 11 and 13); with it none are. The crate's other tests pass

Same sub-pixel shift from either side of an edge

Each crack between two regions is moved by a sub-pixel shift read from the image. The reading looked for a thin run (one to three pixels) on the crack's inside before one on its outside, so where both sides were thin (a one- or two-pixel sliver beside another, as in soft gradients and noisy scans) the two regions of the crack got different shifts, their shared piece different points, and the abutting curves left slivers that no path covers. Layers::shift now averages the reading from either side, (s(c) - s(c flipped)) / 2, which is exactly antisymmetric in floating point, so both regions get the same points. Tests on code-drawn images: a_crack_between_thin_slivers_has_the_same_shift_from_either_side (a noisy ripple of 1–2 px columns at three thresholds: every crack's shift is the exact negative of its flipped crack's, and the image does have cracks the one-sided reading gets wrong) and, in tests/shared_boundaries.rs, thin_slivers_on_both_sides_of_an_edge_leave_nothing_uncovered (a 48 px noisy ripple in Black and White at thresholds 140, 157, 170 and Paths 0, 50, 100, sampled on the 4 × 4 grid per pixel the shared-boundary check uses). With the one-sided reading 482–1,228 sample points per trace were uncovered; now none are. The crate's other tests pass unchanged.

A simpler contour walk

contour::trace_mask follows each region's outline along the pixel cracks with the inside on the right. It used to build a table of outgoing directions for every pixel corner in a raster pass and then follow it. It now works out each turn during the walk from the two pixels ahead of the current corner (right if the one ahead on the right is outside, straight if the one ahead on the left is, else left; so at a saddle it turns right and diagonal pixels stay apart, as before). Loop starts are found by scanning the pixels for a top edge on the outline, with one flag per pixel for a top edge already walked. A mask of the wrong length now gives no loops instead of failing an assertion. The output is identical: the_walk_equals_the_stored_direction_walk compares it with a test-only copy of the previous walk on all 65,536 masks of 4 × 4 pixels and on 400 seeded 23 × 17 masks. Time (best of 7, release build, 4 cores): the walk alone on 2000 × 2000 masks 19.1 ms vs 26.6 ms (a smooth pattern), 81.3 vs 93.2 ms (12% random pixels), 223.1 vs 238.1 ms (50% random pixels); whole traces of 1000 × 1000 images 73.1 vs 77.1 ms (Black and White), 249.6 vs 251.7 ms (Color, 6 colours) and 214.1 vs 218.6 ms (Color, 6 colours, Noise 1), with the same segment counts.

Noise threshold stability (local development, 2026-10-14)

Noise must merge whole 4-connected regions, including pixels absorbed by an earlier merge. A count of regions alone cannot establish this: one old region may split while others disappear. The repeated-pass implementation at a0671777 fails a partition regression at case 0, Noise 3.

The replacement discovers the initial regions, processes them in deterministic increasing-area order, and keeps their membership current. All adjacent regions of the chosen colour join the merge, with ties resolved by label and region id. Increasing Noise continues the same merge sequence: every lower-threshold region remains wholly inside one higher-threshold region. Regions exactly at the threshold stay; an opaque island surrounded by transparency stays; transparent pixels are never recoloured. A merged region is tested at its current area, so a 1-pixel speck joining a 2-pixel region meets Noise 3 and is retained as one 3-pixel region.

Tests generate 64 multicolour 7 × 7 grids with transparent barriers and check every threshold from 0 through 24 with an independent component traversal. They assert nested partitions, non-increasing region counts and unchanged transparency. Separate cases check the strict threshold, isolated opaque islands and invalid or overflowing grid dimensions. The existing no-small-region and traced-path regressions remain active.

This uses region and heap bookkeeping instead of repeated full-image passes, so memory and photo quality must also be checked. Local photo/scan validation uses fixed hashed inputs and identical settings on unchanged current source and the candidate, including separate portrait crops and white-composited PNG comparisons. These local fixtures are not repository assets. The parity score is unchanged.

Pieces returning to a junction

An outline piece can return to its starting junction while being fitted as an open chain. At loose Corners/Paths settings, a nonzero positioned boundary could collapse to one cubic and lose a small corner-touching mark. Fixed-end pieces with identical endpoints and fewer than two fitted segments now retain their positioned boundary when its area is finite and nonzero. Fitting the shared piece this way also gives an abutting fill exactly the reversed boundary, including within a compound path. Six source-generated regressions check 1- and 2-pixel regions through four rotations, both windings and large translations; fixed junctions; zero positioned area; free ends; Noise controls; and shared edges through four rotations and black/white inversion. Noise 2 still removes the 1-pixel mark. The white-hole Noise check is a control, not a reproduced returning-hole case. Defaults are unchanged; these tests establish survival and edge consistency, not a new artistic-quality or parity score. This is separate from the closed-contour recovery in #2012 and straightening guard in #2025.

Thin closed outlines with Snap Curves To Lines

A four-pixel stair-step mark can fit as two straight cubics along the same diagonal, leaving a closed path with zero area. The outline-distance checks accept this because both sides are close to the chord. Closed fitting now checks analytic curve area after straightening and upright levelling; if a nondegenerate source loop has lost its interior, it retains the positioned boundary as an unsimplified polygon. The area integral is translated to a local origin to reduce cancellation. This numerical degeneracy check is not a new Noise or minimum-feature-area setting.

fit::snap_area_tests covers the mark at two positions and five Corners settings, a white stair-step hole, reversed/rotated closed boundaries with exactly matching opposite curves, and a narrow filled rectangle. Noise still removes the four-pixel mark or hole when set to 5. It does not change open-piece fitting, global Snap tolerances or defaults, and it does not address every possible zero-area compound outline. The preset quality table below is the previous measurement, not a rerun of that full report.

Tiny closed contours

A coarse fit can reduce a small closed outline to one cubic, which the editable-path builder cannot represent as a closed subpath with two anchors. Previously that dropped the component or hole after it survived Noise. The fitter now retains the positioned contour without further simplification when that minimum is not met. Corners and Noise defaults are unchanged. Three source-generated regressions cover a one-pixel island, a one-pixel hole with opposite winding, and a stepped seven-pixel region across Corners values. They also check that Noise still removes the island and fills the hole when its threshold requests that. These are survival and winding checks, not a new quality-harness baseline or a general claim about small-feature fidelity.
